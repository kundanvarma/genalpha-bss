3 · The product owner

Catalog (console → Product Offerings / Specifications / Prices)

Offerings are TMF620 data: hard and soft bundles (bundledProductOfferingOption cardinality, pick-N-of-M choice groups), characteristic-conditioned prices ("+2.00/mo when colour = Titanium"), commitment terms, categories that drive placement and fulfilment (Top-ups and Insurance are billing-only; Partner services mint entitlement codes; everything else draws an MSISDN + SIM). Artwork rides the offering's attachments — internal store or the tenant's own PIM; the internal store's bytes live in-row by default or in S3-protocol / Azure Blob object storage (deployment config, §9 — suite #54 proves all three).

Catalog lifecycle — launch is a decision, not a side effect

The TMF620 ladder has server-side TEETH: non-staff callers only ever see offerings that are Launched/Active and inside their validFor window — the list filter is forced, a draft is 404 by id, and ordering one (including a bundle child) is refused at validation. Per-tenant switch catalog-governance (operator form / console Operators pane): direct (default) keeps create-is-live; governed lands every create at "In design" and enforces the ladder one rung at a time (In study → In design → In test → Launched → Retired → Obsolete; pre-launch states shuffle freely). validFor on the offering is the shelf: before the window it is invisible and unorderable, after it it is gone — enforced at query time, no tick decides visibility. When a Launched offering's window opens, ProductOfferingLaunchedEvent fires exactly once for launch-day journeys. Staff walk the unlaunched shelf in the real storefront with ?preview=1 — a PREVIEW badge on everything not yet live. Suite lifecycle_test proves the whole ladder on a throwaway operator.

Rules & pricing as data (console → Rules)

Order rules and dynamic pricing are JSON-logic rows with a dry-run panel. A price change is a rule, not a release. See docs/product-rules.md for the authoring guide.

Product Copilot (console → Copilot)

Chat a product into the catalog: the model proposes specs/prices/offerings as TMF620 payloads, the console shows a human card, and a deterministic executor applies it with your token on confirmation. The model never writes. The copilot also authors pricing rules ("10% off the phone with this plan") and — suite #61 — personalization experience rules ("device browsers see this banner and this pin"): both become policy rows you can disable in Rules. Voice input: the 🎤 button uses the browser's speech recognizer (Chrome/Edge; hidden where unsupported) — the transcript lands in the input and the human still presses Send; the mic never auto-sends. Server-side STT (bring-your-own Whisper) is a named seam for later.

Product Advisor (console → Product advisor) — suite #52

Findings with receipts: top-up attach counted from inventory ("2 of 2 customers on this plan also bought top-ups"), market price gaps from the tenant's market feed (10% materiality threshold — small gaps are deliberately silent), an LLM narrative that never invents a number. Adopt as draft… births an In study offering; nothing reaches the shop until you promote it.

Campaigns & growth (console → Campaigns / Journeys / Audiences)

Segments form from consented shop behavior; campaigns prove lift against real holdouts; journeys read the customer before speaking; A/B arms split deterministically; guardrails (frequency caps, quiet hours) are settings. Every message lands on the TMF683 timeline.

4 · The CSR

Search the 360 (multi-word, name-ranked, with a trigram typo net — "Solvieg" finds Solveig when strict matching finds nothing), then act — every action logs itself on the customer's timeline:

The workspace — who is this, what is happening, what next

The customer page is a cockpit, not a filing cabinet. Under the identity line come three small cards — open tickets, orders in flight, the last contact — then the lines and their health, then money (usage, agreements, bills, promotions and cards), then orders and visits, then the timeline. Sections with nothing in them fold into one line of chips ("Nothing on file: spend policies · household pool · auto top-up off"); a section that holds a warning — a barred line, an open dispute, a paused service — is always open. The tertiary line actions (transfer, change number, reset PIN) sit behind More…; Cease never hides, because a control you cannot see is a control you cannot judge.

The chrome is one bar under 60 px, and every network incident is one line — count, the newest names, services affected, since when — with details on click and a dismiss that lasts the session (a new incident brings it back). Whether an incident touches this customer is said on the customer's page, not in the banner.

GenAlpha Assist sits beside every customer and is grounded or it is nothing. Its situation (an open problem on one of this customer's services, a paused line, an open bill) and its recommended action come from the operational ontology (GET /ontology/v1/context/customer/{id}/recommendations): a governed action such as resuming a paused line or moving a plan up within its family is dry-run first and shows its conditions, permission and policy answer under Why; Do it executes it with the agent's own rights and logs the interaction; Not relevant is recorded for desk learning. Below it the copilot's summary and an offer to consider (on request, never auto-run), and the knowledge the situation asks for — preview inline or Send it to the customer's inbox, logged.

The search box takes anything the caller reads out — a name, an email, a phone number, or an order / ticket / customer / product id — and lands on the customer, saying how it got there; the six customers opened last are chips above the results. The ticket queue is a dense list (severity, issue, customer name, age, time in state, status) with the selected ticket worked in a panel beside it.

The loop. Every recommendation Assist shows is a logged decision of the BSS (ontology.recommend in the decision log). Do it, Not relevant and the thumbs go back as its outcome, and the next ranking reads that history for the desk: a recommendation agents keep rejecting is ranked down, one they keep taking is ranked up — and Assist says so under the recommendation ("shown 43 times on this desk; taken 1, dismissed 21 — ranked down"). On Chats, every new customer message is read for intent (a chip with its confidence, a one-line summary, a reply to consider that the agent sends or rewrites). At the end of a call, Draft the after-call note writes the wrap-up from what the record shows since the page opened; the agent edits it and logs it. Keyboard: ⌘K opens the command palette (pages, recent customers, every action on the page); / t c k n r ? when the caret is not in a field. Suites #126 and #127 (scenario KPIs); design notes in docs/csr-workspace.md.

The knowledge base at the desk

Articles are audience-shelved (customer / CSR / sales / product owner) and the CSR's ask-with-sources grounds its answers in them. Search is full-text, ranked, and stems in the tenant's own language — the locale that picks the storefront's currency also picks the stemmer, so on nova "regning" finds "regningene" (suite #55). Typos in customer search are caught by a trigram net that only speaks when strict matching finds nothing — strict results are byte-identical to before. Beneath the keyword search sits the semantic net (pgvector): "why is my internet so slow" finds the fair-use article that contains neither word, and nonsense finds nothing — the cosine ceiling keeps the net honest. Embeddings are a seam: bss.knowledge.embeddings = stub (deterministic, keyless) or openai-compatible (any real model).

5 · Billing & money operations

The billing run

POST /tmf-api/customerBillManagement/v4/billingRun (staff). Segment-based and per-account: mid-month starts, mid-cycle plan changes and ceases each bill exactly their own days, labelled ("from the 16th, 15 days"). Payday alignment per customer (POST /individual/{id}/billingCycle, day 1–28). The invariant the suites enforce from every direction: no day is ever billed twice.

The migration rehearsal — the parallel run before anyone moves

POST /migrationRehearsal (billing, billing:admin) takes a legacy export as rows {externalRef, offeringName, expectedMonthly} and prices each against THIS catalog with the same engine that cuts real bills: matched within tolerance, price-differs by exactly how much, or offering-missing — the exceptions BY NAME, an honest readyToCutOver flag that is only true when the exception lists are empty, and a read-only promise in the assumptions (the persisted report is the only write). The scariest sentence in a BSS sale — "we'll migrate you" — becomes a report. Suite migration_rehearsal_test.

The shadow-operator clone — simulate by running the real thing

POST /onboarding/v1/operator/{sourceId}/clone {id} (host desk) mints a sandbox operator that IS the source, again: same form-onboarding machinery, the source's catalog and rules copied over the tenants' own staff tokens (ids remapped), and sandbox: "true" stamped in its fleet block — attested in the gateway manifest so no channel can mistake it for production. The wall: a sandbox tenant can never reach the outside world — the communication egress (email/SMS/push, prospect raw-email included) stores the message in-app marked sandbox-suppressed, never delivered. The answer: GET /portfolioDiff?tenantA&tenantB prices both shelves with the same engine that cuts real bills — matched offerings with per-name deltas (a price move ripples through every bundle that carries it), only-in lists, portfolio totals, assumptions on its face. Mutate the clone, read the diff, throw the clone away: simulation with zero model drift. The clone has since grown the full simulation substrate: Tvilling base seeding (seedTwinBase — the only thing read from the source is the aggregate offering distribution; the clone mints proportional synthetic subscribers, fictional by construction, sandbox-only by guard), the wall extended to every door (PSP sessions and card charges refused, ad-platform activations refused, the GA4 seam silent in both directions), wholesale rate cards travelling with the copy, and time compression: a per-tenant clock seam (clock-offset-days, sandbox-gated structurally) that billing periods, dunning's overdue decisions and usage's wholesale windows all read — simulateQuarter bills three compressed months in minutes, no day billed twice. Suite shadow_clone_test, twelve legs.

The commercial simulator, complete — five answers off the real engines

Prospect simulator (POST /onboarding/v1/prospectSimulation): a prospect's PUBLIC price list + an assumed mix → a fresh walled sandbox, their shelf, a twin base, a billed compressed quarter — "your business on our BSS" in the first meeting, touching none of their data. Chaos twin (GET /chaosReport?scenario=psp-outage&days=N): a failure mode priced in currency off the real ledger — revenue at risk, bills hit, how many age beyond terms before the outage ends; clock-composable in a clone. Regulatory rehearsal (GET /ai/v1/priceRiseRehearsal): the right-to-exit window counted before any letter exists — the cohort IS the notification list, port-out exposure = churn-flagged holders, upside vs annual-at-risk, with the honesty line that a score is the model's opinion, not fate. Elasticity flywheel: the price simulator's default prior is MEASURED (lifetime ceased products over base), labeled a floor not a price response, always overridable. Cross-tenant priors (GET /ai/v1/elasticityBenchmark): each tenant's baseline computed inside its own context, only the unnamed distribution leaves, and below the k-anonymity floor of three the benchmark refuses to exist. Suites prospect_sim_test, chaos_twin_test, reg_rehearsal_test, elasticity_benchmark_test.

6 · Partner channels — suites #48/#51

Being a dealer is a row. Sign a chain: POST /dealer/v1/agreement (staff) with the org, commission per activation, and — for a chain with its own POS — the OAuth2 clientId that speaks for them. Clerks are org membership; foreign dealers see 404-shaped nothing.

Retail (the CSP + external-retail model — think Elkjøp/Power)

Counter sale: sell against the customer's email; the order carries the dealer stamp. Starter kits: mint batches (code + SIM + attribution in the box); the customer self-activates (POST /dealer/v1/starterKit/activate) and the line rides the SIM from the box. POS API: same endpoints, machine credential, per-partner rate limits at the edge (429 + Retry-After); the chain's own phone rides the sale as device context — never a billable item. Commission: pending → earned after the angrerett window → clawed back with the reason if the customer leaves inside it.

Telesales (outbound, shaped by the law)

Dial list: GET /dealer/v1/telesales/dialList?segment= — consented at the source (insight segments), every number DNC-washed, reserved citizens excluded and counted. The call makes an OFFER (POST /dealer/v1/telesales/offer): washed fail-closed (reserved refuses, unreachable register refuses, unconfigured tenant refuses); no order exists yet. The customer confirms in writing (POST /telesales/v1/confirm, signed in, code from their inbox — or, for a cold prospect, after registering with the offered email); only then the order, the activation and the commission are born. Silence expires on a clock. Every dialer call logs to TMF683.

8 · AI configuration & governance — suites #53/#59

The individualized shop (suite #60)

GET /ai/v1/forYou — the signed-in customer's own rail (self-scoped: party = token subject, no parameter to probe). TMF680 candidates + consented interests + churn-risk loyalty flag; ONE governed FAST caption per 5-minute cache window ("Picked for you after your look at devices"). The control plane (suite #59)

Every LLM turn is metered (token counts + cost land on the AI Audit ledger — the console's AI Audit tab shows tokens, cost and outcome per turn) and governed per tenant: GET /ai/v1/governance shows spend this window, the ceiling, remaining headroom and the agent-action trail (which AI wrote which resource — e.g. the advisor's catalog.createDraftOffering). Cost is estimated (chars/4 tokens × per-model rate, bss.ai.prices.<model> in micros per 1K, default bss.ai.default-price-per-1k-micros); exact provider usage is a wired-later seam.

13 · Production operations — suites #56/#57

Ticks that never double-fire

Every mutating scheduled job — dunning, bill delivery, campaign journeys, churn sweeps, porting cutover, commission hardening, order resume, telesales expiry, cart abandonment — claims a row-lease (tick_lock table in the service's own database) before it runs. Scale any service to N replicas and each tick still fires once; a crashed holder's lease expires on its own. Inspect a service's leases with psql -d billing -c "SELECT * FROM tick_lock". Suite #56 proves it by running TWO billing replicas and counting exactly one delivered bill at the receiving partner.

The billing run — partitioned, resumable, and on a ledger

Each account's bill commits in its own transaction; a failed account is recorded and skipped, never the run. A crashed run RESUMES on the next trigger — every bill already cut is its own checkpoint — and every run is a row: GET /tmf-api/customerBillManagement/v4/billingRun lists recent runs (status running/completed/superseded, accounts, bills, failures, last error). One run speaks at a time: a trigger during a live run answers {"busy": true} (the lease heartbeats per account, so a crashed run frees it within ~2 minutes), and the database enforces one bill per account per period with a unique index — constraint, not convention. Suite #57 kills billing mid-run and proves exactly-one-bill-per-customer after the resume. BSS_BILLING_RUN_ACCOUNT_DELAY_MS (default 0) is the dev pacing knob the suite uses to hold a run open.

Load numbers and the smoke SLO

Who this is for: an operator or a fork bringing the BSS to a new country/market and asking two questions — how do I switch on what already ships (Helthjem, Posten/Bring, PostNord, Klarna, PayPal…) and how do I add a provider you never built (DHL, PostNL, a national registry)? Tier 1 — BIND a shipped provider (config, no code, no fork). Every shipped adapter is dormant until a tenant binds it with a config row: PUT the row (idempotent upsert), or use the console's Integrations cards (Logistics / Payment). secretRef is an env-var NAME, never a key — going live is pointing baseUrl at the real API and setting one environment variable on the service:

  -H "Authorization: Bearer $TOKEN" -H "Content-Type: application/json" -d '{
    "carrier": "bring", "displayName": "Posten/Bring",
    "baseUrl": "https://api.bring.com/booking",      # real API = go-live
    "secretRef": "BRING_API_KEY",                    # name of the env var, never the key

    "provider": "klarna", "baseUrl": "...", "secretRef": "KLARNA_API_KEY",
    "webhookSecretRef": "KLARNA_WEBHOOK_SECRET", "methods": ["klarna"], "priority": 10 }'

Tier 2 — WIRE an unshipped carrier through the generic connector (still config only). Fulfilment ships an HttpCarrierAdapter where config is the adapter: any carrier whose API takes a JSON booking and returns a tracking reference is mapped with a path + JSON pointers — proven by suite carrier_generic_test.js (#101), which points it at the Bring-shaped mock, a wire it has zero code for, and books a real order:

    "carrier": "http", "displayName": "PostNL",
    "baseUrl": "https://api.postnl.example", "secretRef": "POSTNL_API_KEY",
    "methods": ["home","pickupPoint"],
    "config": {
      "bookPath":        "/v2/shipments",            # POST target for a booking
      "trackingPointer": "/shipment/reference",      # JSON pointers map the reply
      "shipmentPointer": "/shipment/id",
      "urlPointer":      "/shipment/trackingUrl",
      "labelPointer":    "/shipment/labelRef",
      "pickupPath":      "/v2/locations?postcode={postcode}",
      "pointsPointer":   "/locations" } }'

The safety boundary is deliberate: money never rides the generic seam — a failed carrier booking degrades to the manual warehouse flow, so carriers are safe to generic-connect; PSPs are named adapters only. 42 · Collections — the statutory ladder

What: the path from a missed payment to a restricted, suspended, or cured customer — shaped by national debt-collection and telecom law, not by a retry loop. Lives in billing (the case + policy + money) and service-orchestration (the enforcement primitives). Suite collections_test.js. The case: one collectionCase per financial account (it aggregates ALL overdue bills — rung actions fire once per account, never once per invoice), state machine CURRENT → REMINDED → WARNED → RESTRICTED → SUSPENDED with orthogonal holds — PROMISE_TO_PAY (pauses the ladder; a broken promise resumes it), DISPUTE_HOLD (freezes only the disputed amount — the rest still ages), HARDSHIP_HOLD (manual) — and CURED reachable from any state the moment the actionable balance drops under threshold. The policy and its floor: dunningPolicy per tenant (ordered steps: offset days, template, enforcement, fee), sitting on a country statutory pack it cannot undercut — the Norway pack pins the 14-day reminder-fee gate, the kr 38 fee cap, at most two fee-bearing reminders, the one-month enforcement-notice clock and the 250 minimum actionable floor. Cure auto-resumes within the sweep and posts a reconnection fee (tenant config, contractual — zeroable). Fees post as rate lines through the existing AR machinery, gated by the pack. 45 · Usage policy — pools, caps & the roaming wall
